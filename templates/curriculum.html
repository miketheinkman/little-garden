From a first try to a next step

Find a starting point
Choose an untimed check-in for numbers, keyboard use or word sounds, or assign an initial placement in a specific activity. A complete check with evidence establishes a baseline. For hands-on activities, you can record what you saw.
Read the evidence
Independent first answers show accuracy. A retry, skip or adult-assisted solve tells a different story and stays visible. Coloring can measure coverage and puzzles record hints. Guided tracing records participation. Keyboard pace can refine the estimate after three correct first tries.
Choose the next round
Tiers 1–5 describe task difficulty within a skill. The library compares a lesson with your child’s current estimate and labels its fit. Your queue comes first. When it’s empty, suggestions favor skills that need practice and stay within one tier of the estimate.
Revisit a skill
A review is suggested after four weeks, or after eight later fully scored rounds in that skill area. Open Skill details to see the individual letters, numbers or concepts behind a score. A “Demonstrated” target averages at least 80% across recent independent attempts from at least two rounds.

Want to spend a week on M and S? A practice focus can keep a round short or mix those letters with familiar ones. Focus is optional. The three multi-tier check-ins keep their standard mix so you can compare results over time.
